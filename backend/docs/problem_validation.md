# Confirming the ammonia problem in Kenya (before building at scale)

Goal: in 3–4 weeks, collect evidence that night-time ammonia is a real, costly problem
for Kenyan semi-commercial poultry farmers, and that they would act on alerts.
This evidence is what innovation-centre panels, grant reviewers and investors ask for.

## 1. Who to talk to (aim for 15+ farmers, 3+ experts)

* Farmers with 300–10,000 broilers or layers in closed deep-litter houses
  (Kiambu, Nakuru, Machakos, Kajiado, Uasin Gishu, Kakamega). Not free-range.
* County livestock / veterinary officers and private poultry vets.
* Agrovet shop owners (they hear about sick flocks first).
* Hatchery / feed company field extension staff.

## 2. Farmer interview (15 minutes, Swahili or English)

1. How many birds do you keep? Broilers or layers? How is the house built (curtains, walls, roof)?
2. What do you do with the curtains at night? Why? Does it change with the age of the birds?
3. When you enter the house early in the morning, how does the air smell? Do your eyes sting?
4. Have your birds had breathing problems (sneezing, rattling, swollen eyes)? How often?
5. What did the vet or agrovet say it was? What did treatment cost last cycle?
6. How many birds died last cycle? In which week did most die?
7. How often do you change or turn the litter? What happens near the drinkers?
8. Do you have a fan or electricity in the house? Power cuts?
9. If your phone got an SMS at 9 pm saying "ammonia will be dangerous by 2 am, open the curtain 30 % for 30 minutes", would you do it? Why or why not?
10. Would you pay for this? How much per month, or per cycle? (Don't suggest a number first.)

Write down exact quotes. Don't pitch the solution until question 9.

## 3. Measure, don't only ask (the strongest evidence)

Borrow or rent an ammonia meter (electrochemical handheld) or use colourimetric gas
detector tubes, or install your first prototype node next to a reference meter.
Visit 5–10 houses at three times: **midday, 10 pm, 5–6 am**. Measure at bird height
(about 30 cm) near drinkers and in the centre.

| Farm | Birds | Age (days) | Curtain at night | NH3 midday | NH3 10 pm | NH3 5 am | Temp 5 am | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Problem confirmed if:** at least half of the houses exceed 20–25 ppm at night or early
morning for birds older than ~3 weeks, and farmers report respiratory disease or losses.

## 4. Experts: questions

* Which respiratory problems do you see most in broilers/layers here? In which months?
* How much of it do you link to poor ventilation / ammonia?
* What advice do you give farmers about curtains at night? Do they follow it?

## 5. Turn it into a one-page problem statement

* Who: semi-commercial farmers, N birds, in counties X, Y.
* Pain: measured ammonia levels, % of farms over the limit, losses and treatment costs.
* Root cause: warmth vs ventilation trade-off at night, invisible gas, no measurement.
* Why AI + IoT: sensors make the gas visible 24/7; AI warns hours ahead and recommends
  the least-cold action, specific to each house — a fixed alarm can't do that.
* Evidence: interviews, measurements, expert quotes.
